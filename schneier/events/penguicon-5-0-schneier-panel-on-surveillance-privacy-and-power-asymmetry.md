## Event

Bruce Schneier appeared as Tech Guest of Honor at Penguicon 5.0, held April 20-22, 2007, at the Holiday Inn in Livonia, Michigan.

The conversation took place at **"The Future of Spyware"** — Saturday, April 21, 2007, 11AM-noon, Ballroom A — a panel with Schneier and Charlie Stross. Mark Ramm was in attendance. Ramm had initially misremembered Cory Doctorow as the co-panelist; the program confirms it was Stross. (Doctorow was Penguicon 3.0 GoH in 2005 and was not listed in the 2007 program. Schneier and Doctorow had just won the EFF Pioneer Award together six weeks earlier — March 27, 2007 — which likely accounts for the conflation.)

## Intellectual Content — Primary Source

The following represents Ramm's firsthand account of Schneier's argument as expressed at this panel:

Schneier articulated a direct counter to David Brin's *The Transparent Society* (1998). Where Brin proposed mutual surveillance as an egalitarian solution — if everyone can watch everyone, power is equalized — Schneier argued that surveillance systematically *increases* power asymmetries rather than equalizing them. The institutions with the most power have the greatest capacity to resist being watched (legal tools to classify, redact, deny, transfer) while simultaneously expanding their capacity to watch others. Surveillance therefore amplifies existing inequalities rather than correcting them.

Schneier's explicit framing, as Ramm understood it: **privacy is essential to egalitarian society**. The erosion of privacy does not create transparency — it creates a one-way mirror in which the powerful see without being seen.

This is the argument that *Data and Goliath* (2015) develops at book length, but the Penguicon panel captures it in the sharper, more direct formulation that preceded the published version by eight years.

Schneier also articulated a **data-as-pollution** metaphor in approximately this period: data as a byproduct of activity that accumulates, persists, enters systems it was never intended for, and causes harm the original actor never anticipated — with those least able to opt out most exposed to its effects.

## Significance

This is a primary source for the Schneier → Ramm intellectual lineage on surveillance and power asymmetry. The data-as-pollution metaphor and the privacy-as-egalitarianism argument together form the upstream inputs for Ramm's [[asymmetric-visibility]] concept and the cascade research's treatment of surveillance infrastructure.

The Brin counter is stated more starkly in conversation than in Schneier's published work, where it is implicit in the power-asymmetry analysis of *Data and Goliath* and the [[trust-framework]] critique of the Crypto Wars settlement.
